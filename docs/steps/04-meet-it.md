# Meet It

**Get to know the people who will use it: what they're trying to get done,
and what frustrates them today.**

Apps are built for people, so Meet It puts them first. You describe one to
three kinds of people (we call them *personas*): who they are, what
they're trying to do, what gets in their way, and where they'll be when
they use your app. From here on, every feature and every piece of work
is written *for* one of them.

## What happens

Claude asks about real people you know who have the problem. Often there's
more than one side to it: someone who creates and someone who receives,
someone who asks and someone who approves. For each persona:

| | Example: SlideIt's Audience Member |
|---|---|
| **Trying to get done** | Follow the talk without squinting at a screen across the room. |
| **Frustrations today** | Can't read the slides from the back; never opens the link sent afterwards. |
| **Context** | Standing or sitting in a crowd, on their phone, in the moment. |

**The device matters.** Whether someone's on a phone in a crowd or at a
desk with a big screen shapes how each part of the app is designed and
how it's tested.

## What you end up with

- Your personas in `PRODUCT.md`.
- A **Who it's for** section at the top of your project's front page on
  GitHub, so anyone opening it meets your people first.
- Tests set up for the devices your people actually use.

## What it asks of you

| | |
|---|---|
| **Time** | 15-20 minutes |
| **Decisions** | Who the app is really for, and who can wait |
| **Cost** | Nothing |

## Come back when you learn something

Your first personas are your best guess. Once real people use the app,
you'll often discover someone you didn't expect, or find that someone
isn't quite who you thought. Run Meet It again to add or adjust them.
It then offers a Dream It session focused on the newcomer, and a Trim It
to decide what's worth building for them next.
